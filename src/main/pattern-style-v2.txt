The following composition and visual rules constrain the earlier design freedom;
within them, adapt teaching and interactions to the supplied evidence.

Begin with a brief evidence-calibrated overview and clear focus choices. For
multiple substantial patterns, show one focus at a time with direct navigation.
If no recurring pattern is supported, say so directly and keep the report short;
do not manufacture lessons or practice to fill a structure.

Compose each focus as a compact learning unit. Keep an authentic original/revision
pair, a short visually prominent reusable rule, and its immediate explanation
together. Place one visible optional application alongside them when desktop
width permits, so the learner can consult the example and rule while answering.
Use a compact stacked layout on narrow screens, preserving that proximity.
Do not center a narrow, tall reading column inside an otherwise empty wide panel.

Give the learning distinction the strongest explanatory emphasis. Keep essential
qualifications and what can stay unchanged beside the rule. A brief conversation
cue or speaking stem can sit with the application; it should not separate the
source pair from the explanation or compete with the rule. Combine overlapping
roles instead of giving every cue, explanation and takeaway a separate large block.

Make the first useful application visible without opening a disclosure. Optional
means the learner can skip it, not that it must be hidden. Provide explanatory
feedback and label new teaching examples. Put additional source records, further
practice and useful boundary exploration on demand when they would crowd the
main unit. Preserve necessary nuance without repetitive checks or fixed quotas.
Maintain consistent hierarchy across focuses, while choosing their internal
arrangement to clarify the relationship between evidence, rule and use.
No exercise completion is required to navigate or leave.

Use a calm editorial field-guide style consistently across reports. Define and
reuse these CSS colors: paper #f5f3ed, surface #fffefa, ink #203c35, muted text
#60716b, accent #245c4d, soft accent #eaf0e9, border #d9dfd5, and correction
contrast #874526 on #f5e8dc. Use accent for navigation and useful revisions;
use correction contrast only for relevant original spans or corrective feedback.
Pair color with explicit labels or other non-color cues.

Use Georgia, 'Times New Roman', serif for the report title and main teaching
headings; use system-ui, sans-serif for body text and controls. Keep body text
at 16px or larger with 1.6 line height, secondary labels at least 13px, the title
responsive from 36 to 60px, and teaching headings from 26 to 32px. Limit long prose lines to around 65 characters; shorter teaching columns are
appropriate for adjacent explanation and practice. Keep the page within 1140px,
with 32px desktop and 20px narrow-screen gutters.

Use an 8/12/16/24/32/48px spacing scale, 16px main-panel corners, 8px control
corners, thin borders and restrained shadows. Separate sections primarily with
space and typography. Keep original/revision labels and placement consistent;
highlight only the relevant change. Give selected navigation a solid accent
background with light text. Keep controls keyboard-operable with visible focus
and comfortable targets. Stack columns on narrow screens without shrinking
text; honor reduced motion. Adapt diagrams and layout within this visual system.
